Mater Dei — Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 24, 2026

Mater Dei is a Catholic prayer app for praying the Holy Rosary. It is built to be prayed with, not to gather data. This policy describes, completely, what the app does and does not do with information.

The short version

The Mater Dei app collects no personal data. None. The app has no accounts, no sign-ins, no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind. It never sends your name, your intentions, or anything else you enter or it remembers to us or to anyone else. We operate no servers, and we never sell or share anyone's personal data.

The sections below say exactly what other companies can see when the app fetches sacred media or reads a prayer aloud, and what reaches us if you buy the app or write to us.

Who we are

Mater Dei is made by an independent developer: the developer named on the app's App Store and Google Play pages. The Mac version, if you have it, comes from the same developer. "We" and "us" in this policy mean that developer, who is responsible for the app and for this policy. Our privacy point of contact is materdei.support@proton.me; more under "Contact and support" at the end of this page.

What stays on your device

Everything you enter or the app remembers is stored only in the app's private storage on your own device:

  • The name you enter for the greeting, and the names in your Prayer Circle
  • Prayer intentions and intention history
  • Streaks and completed-Rosary counts
  • Language, prayer, audio, and accessibility settings
  • Daily reminder time, and the name of your device's time zone when the reminder was set (so the reminder can follow you when you travel)
  • Saved progress within a Rosary

On iPhone and Android, the operating system keeps this storage closed to other apps. The app never sends any of it anywhere, and we operate no servers, so there is no copy for us to access, sell, or lose.

If you tap Copy summary to clipboard in My intentions, the app copies your intentions, each with the number of Rosaries offered for it, to your device's clipboard. From there the list goes wherever you paste it, and to your other devices if your device syncs its clipboard with them. Apart from that, the only way any of this information can leave your device is in a backup you make yourself:

  • Android: automatic app backup and device-to-device transfer are switched off deliberately, so none of it is copied to your Google Drive or to a new phone. The trade-off is that a new phone starts a fresh streak. Uninstalling the app deletes all of it.
  • iPhone (and iPad): the app's data may be included in your own device backup (iCloud or computer). An iCloud backup is kept by Apple under your Apple Account; a backup to a computer is encrypted only if you turn on the Encrypt local backup option. Either way the backup is yours and is never accessible to us. Deleting the app removes the data from the device (offloading the app, which keeps its documents and data, does not); a backup made earlier keeps its copy until that backup is replaced or deleted.
  • Mac: the data stays in the app's folder in your Mac's Library, and your own Time Machine backup may include it. Moving the app to the Trash does not remove that folder, so use Reset to defaults (below) first.

How long it is kept, and how to erase it

The app keeps your information until you erase it, and you can do that at any time without deleting the app:

  • Settings → About → Reset to defaults erases everything listed above and cancels the daily reminder.
  • Settings → Prayer Circle → My intentions removes a single intention from your history, and My prayer circle removes a single name.

The app never asks for your consent to collect data, because it collects none. The one permission it may ask for, notifications, can be withdrawn at any time in your device's settings, or you can simply turn the daily reminder off in Settings.

We hold no copy of your app data, so there is nothing for us to delete and no request you need to make.

Network use

The app itself goes online for one purpose only: fetching public-domain and freely licensed (Creative Commons) sacred media from Wikimedia Commons, over encrypted (HTTPS) connections.

  • Gregorian chant and the completion bell are not bundled with the app. The chant is fetched when you turn it on during prayer, and the bell when you first finish a Rosary. On iPhone and Android each recording is then kept in the app's cache and plays offline without further requests (unless your device clears the cache to free space); on a Mac it may be streamed again each time it plays.
  • The Mystery paintings all ship inside the app. One is fetched from Wikimedia only if its bundled copy cannot be loaded.

(Credits for the Creative Commons recordings and photographs are in the app under Settings → About → Licenses & credits.) These are ordinary web requests: like any web server, Wikimedia's servers see your IP address and the ordinary technical details of the request (such as the software making it and, on some devices, the system version and language) when serving the file. No intention, prayer, setting, or any other app data is included in these requests. Wikimedia's handling of server logs is governed by the Wikimedia Privacy Policy.

When the app reads a prayer with your device's own speech engine, that engine may go online; see "Spoken prayer" below.

This page. Settings → About → Privacy policy opens this page in your web browser; the app sends nothing with it. The page is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub logs each visitor's IP address for security purposes, under the GitHub General Privacy Statement. We have added no analytics, cookies, or scripts to this page.

Spoken prayer

The app includes AI-narrated audio clips of the prayers it reads aloud: voices generated in advance from the public prayer texts, shipped inside the app. While Settings → Prayer → Use AI-narrated voices is on (the default), these clips play from the app itself, and apart from the cases below no text-to-speech engine, voice service, or AI service is involved. The app never contacts the service that made the clips, or any other AI service.

When that setting is off, for a Hail Mary that names the Mystery (the optional Mystery clauses, which no fixed clip can say), or if a clip cannot be loaded, the app uses your device's own text-to-speech engine: Apple's built-in voices on iPhone and Mac, or on Android whichever speech engine is set as the default in your device's settings (for example Google's or Samsung's). The app asks that engine for the best-sounding voice it offers in the prayer's language. On iPhone and Mac, Apple's voices run on the device. On Android, some engines, Google's among them, also offer "network" voices that are synthesized on the engine maker's servers. Mater Dei gives preference to voices whose names mark them as network or online voices (as Google's are named), so when your engine offers one and you are online, the text of the prayer being read can be sent to the engine's maker to be spoken, under that company's own privacy policy. Whether any other voice works online is up to the engine you have set as the default. The app sends the engine only the public text of the prayers themselves, never your name, your intentions, or anything else you enter.

Notifications

The optional daily reminder is scheduled by your device's own notification system, at the time you choose. Its text is fixed (the same for everyone who uses the app in the same language) and never includes your name or intentions. No notification service of ours is involved, because we don't have one, and the app uses no push notifications.

Children

Mater Dei's prayers, paintings, and music are suitable for all ages, but the app is not directed at children or at anyone under 18. Because the app collects no data from anyone, it collects no data from children.

Permissions

The app asks only for what its features need.

  • Notifications (iPhone, Android, and Mac) — the optional daily reminder. The app asks only when you turn the reminder on.

On Android, the system also lists these, granted at install:

  • Internet access — the Wikimedia requests described above.
  • Run at startup — so a scheduled reminder survives a restart.
  • Vibration — added by the notification component so the reminder can vibrate. (The light taps you feel on the beads need no permission.)
  • View network connections — added automatically by the media player component, which reads the connection type (such as Wi-Fi or mobile data) whenever it plays audio, to tune its streaming of the chant and bell. The app itself never reads your network details, and the connection type is never sent anywhere.

It does not request the camera, microphone, location, contacts, or photos on any platform.

Purchases and store reports

You buy Mater Dei through the App Store or Google Play, which handle your payment under their own privacy policies; we never see your payment details. Like every developer, we receive the reports the stores provide: sales and download figures; aggregate usage statistics (such as installs and sessions, by country and device model); ratings and reviews; and, from people who have chosen in their device settings to share diagnostic data (with app developers on Apple devices, or with Google on Android), crash reports. Google Play also keeps a record of each order, which we can look up by order number or by the buyer's email address, for example to issue a refund. All of this comes from the stores, not from the app. It stays in the stores' developer consoles, kept under the stores' own retention rules, and we use it only to run, support, and fix the app.

Changes to this policy

If the app's behavior, or the way we handle support email or store reports, ever changes in a way that affects this policy, the policy will be updated here with a new effective date before the change takes effect.

Contact and support

For help with Mater Dei, questions about this policy, or a privacy request, write to materdei.support@proton.me.

If you write to us, we receive your email address and whatever your message contains. We use them only to answer you; we never add you to a mailing list or share your message. Our mailbox is hosted by Proton, under the Proton Privacy Policy. We keep support email only as long as we need it to help you, and we delete yours whenever you ask.
